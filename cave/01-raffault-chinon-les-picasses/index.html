Ce que c'est · what it is

the plain facts of the wine

Cabernet Franc from Chinon, on the Loire. One slope, Les Picasses: limestone under clay, climbing steeply from the bank of the Vienne.

L'histoire · the story

as the family tells it

The name on the bottle is a woman's, and there is a reason.

In 1947 Pierre Raffault died suddenly. The family still tells it with the same detail: that year the vines had let him hope for the finest vintage of the century. His wife Olga was left with two small children and a vineyard in the Véron, the land cradled between the Loire and the Vienne.

A German prisoner of war, Ernest Zeinninger, had worked on the farm since the war ended. In Pierre's last moments he made a promise: to stand by Olga and the children. He helped her bring in that vintage. Then he stayed, and gave the rest of his life to the vines.

The family calls it a deep friendship, and says he had never understood the war. Olga's son Jean learned the vines beside him.

The house has kept their patience. Les Picasses rests a year or two in great old casks, foudres the size of a small room, and the family says it will keep fifteen years. Olga's granddaughter Sylvie makes it now, with her husband Eric and their son Arnaud.

À table · with it

this week's cheese, and the plate that never changes

A Sainte-Maure de Touraine, the goat's cheese from just up the river.

And the plate that is always on the zinc: ham, olives, bread.

Dans le verre · in the glass

the host's note · clues to smell and taste

Dark cherry and cassis. Pencil lead. A little leather. Chalk at the end.

If you have a tongue, you have the glass. If you haven't, you have the story, and it is the better half.
