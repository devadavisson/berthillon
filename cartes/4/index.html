Les faits · what is known

the plain facts of the wine, checked against its own record · no producer named
A Cabernet Franc from the Loire, Bourgueil — the right-bank commune where the tuffeau limestone comes up under the slope. A family estate in its third generation of winemakers; the founder's grandson built the modern domaine fifty years ago on three hectares, and his son came home twenty years ago. Nineteen hectares now, some twenty-five parcels, all Cabernet Franc, split between two neighbouring appellations and bottled parcel by parcel: sand and gravel on the terrace, limestone on the slope, clay and flint on the clos. Organic-certified for some fifteen years and worked by the lunar calendar, without biodynamic certification. The flagship is the clos: two hectares, south-facing, flint over tuffeau, vines planted in the 1970s, a site the abbey rated first-growth centuries ago. Fully destemmed, no crushing; fermented in open-topped conical wooden vats on native yeast, with gentle foot-treading; first press only; four to six weeks on the skins. Raised a year and a half — the larger part in 400-litre casks, the rest in stoneware jars, a practice of the house for a decade — new wood bought only to replace the old. Alcohol a touch over thirteen percent in 2023.

La note d'Antoine · the host's note

a sensory description · one load-bearing word deliberately left out
Dark fruit, dense and ripe — blackberry, a sour griotte cherry under it, and a little red fruit, strawberry, behind. Then the wood, and it's honest about being wood: a dark-chocolate bitterness, a charred edge with smoke behind it, tar, black liquorice, nothing vanilla-sweet but present, part of the wine's architecture rather than hidden under it. Black pepper. A green note at the back of the nose — pepper of the other kind, a bell pepper cut open, a savoury leaf — that the grape can't help; a violet somewhere in it, faint. The palate is thick and dark, almost slow; it clings to the glass and leaves a warm, black bitterness behind it. The Hachette guide's word for it is *pulpeuse*, fleshy. Tannins firm and fine-grained, no softness yet, a wine that tells you to come back in five years and means it. The finish is long and dark and a little bitter, like the chocolate.
