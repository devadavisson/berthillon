Les faits · what is known

the plain facts of the wine, checked against its own record · no producer named
A Champagne from a grower-producer on the Côte des Blancs, Chardonnay-led — about nine parts in ten — with a tenth of Pinot Noir from a single village in the Marne valley. The Grand Cru villages of the Côte des Blancs anchor the blend; a handful of outlying villages, Premier Cru and below, reach out into the valley. Native yeasts; full malolactic; the reserve wines raised in old 600-litre casks that give no wood to the wine; two to three years on the lees before disgorgement. Dosage five to six grams per litre depending on the disgorgement, extra brut. Organic and biodynamic in the vineyard without certification. The disgorgement date is printed on the back label.

La note d'Antoine · the host's note

a sensory description · one load-bearing word deliberately left out
It arrives as sound before taste: a mousse so fine it reads as a texture, not a fizz — satin drawn over the tongue. Lemon, ripe rather than sharp; then apple, the green kind, bitten; pear beside it; a white flower you'd smell in a hedge in April and couldn't name. The palate has two tempers at once: the energy of a wine on chalk, salt and cut and drive, and a fleshiness most of its neighbours don't have. Ginger, faintly, on the way out. The finish is chalk and sea-salt together, and it holds, and it does not quite let you go.
